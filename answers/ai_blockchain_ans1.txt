QUESTION:
Explain repo rate, SDF and MSF in the context of Indian monetary policy.

ANSWER:
In Indian monetary policy, the **repo rate, SDF and MSF** are key facilities used by the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** to manage liquidity and guide short-term interest rates.

### 1. Repo rate

**Repo** means “repurchase agreement.”

- Under a repo operation, banks borrow short-term funds from the RBI.
- They provide eligible government securities as collateral and agree to repurchase them later.
- The **repo rate** is the interest rate charged by the RBI on such borrowing.
- It is the RBI’s main policy rate and is decided by the **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**.

**Effect of a change:**

- **Repo rate cut:** Bank funding becomes cheaper, which can reduce lending rates, encourage borrowing and support economic activity.
- **Repo rate hike:** Bank funding becomes more expensive, generally raising lending rates and helping contain inflation.

The repo rate therefore acts as the main signal for the broader interest-rate structure in the economy.

---

### 2. Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)

The **SDF** allows banks to deposit surplus funds with the RBI overnight and earn interest.

- The RBI absorbs liquidity from banks.
- Unlike repo or reverse repo transactions, the RBI **does not provide collateral** under the SDF.
- It is primarily used when the banking system has excess liquidity.
- The SDF rate generally forms the **floor of the RBI’s liquidity corridor**.

For example, if banks have more funds than they can profitably lend, they may park the surplus with the RBI through the SDF.

---

### 3. Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)

The **MSF** allows eligible banks to borrow overnight funds from the RBI, usually when they face an urgent or unexpected liquidity shortfall.

- Banks borrow against eligible government securities.
- They may also use a limited portion of their statutory liquidity holdings, subject to RBI rules.
- The MSF rate is generally **higher than the repo rate**.
- It forms the **ceiling of the RBI’s liquidity corridor**.

Because it is relatively expensive, banks normally use the MSF as a last-resort or emergency source of funds.

---

### Relationship among the three

A simplified representation of the operating corridor is:

```text
MSF rate       — Upper bound; banks borrow from RBI
Repo rate      — Main policy rate
SDF rate       — Lower bound; banks deposit with RBI
```

The overnight interbank call money rate is generally expected to move within this corridor.

### In summary

| Facility | What happens? | Collateral? | Main purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Repo** | Banks borrow from RBI | Yes | Main policy instrument and liquidity injection |
| **SDF** | Banks deposit funds with RBI | No | Absorb surplus liquidity |
| **MSF** | Banks borrow urgently from RBI | Yes | Provide overnight emergency liquidity |

Together, these facilities help the RBI control short-term liquidity, anchor market interest rates and pursue its inflation and growth objectives.
